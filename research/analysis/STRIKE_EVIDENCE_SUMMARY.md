So the bullish→bottom/bearish→top pairing is real and recurs (7 clean instances across the file) but is explicitly NOT positioned by the speaker as an unconditional formula — it is a heuristic applied when the first candle has clear directional character, with an explicit fallback (wait for the second candle) when it doesn't.

2. **The two "first candle" mechanisms — RESOLVED. They are the same mechanism, not two competing ones.** This was the single biggest open question from the partial review, and the second-pass reading resolves it decisively via three separate pieces of direct evidence:

He also states "we already gave the formula [in an earlier video]," confirming this is a taught, named, reusable procedure, not an improvisation.
   - **Row 14** (line ~4090) is the most decisive: the speaker explicitly warns that the raw NIFTY spot candle can look bullish while the computed Weekly-Future candle for the same period looks bearish — proving that "first candle" in his strike-selection vocabulary always means the **computed Weekly-Future candle**, never the raw index/spot candle, even when he says "top and bottom" in plain language without mentioning the Future explicitly (as in rows 1-6). Rows 1-6's "top/bottom" language and row 8's "compute a synthetic Weekly-Future High/Low" language describe the **same underlying quantity** at two different levels of explicitness — the plain-language rows are simply omitting the "and this comes from the option-derived Weekly Future, not the spot chart" step that other rows spell out.
